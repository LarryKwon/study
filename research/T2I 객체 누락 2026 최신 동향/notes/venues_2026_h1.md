## Q1. Which ICLR / CVPR / ICML 2026 papers address omission, many-object/long-prompt generation, compositional alignment, conflict/counterfactual/negation, object hallucination, counting?

### Takeaway
The first-half-2026 venues accepted a dense cluster of papers on **omission specifically**. ICML 2026 has OSI (omission signal in MM-DiT text tokens), "When Do Diffusion Models Learn to Generate Multiple Objects?" and Divide-and-Denoise (spotlight). ICLR 2026 has CO3 (mode collisions → dropped concepts) and PRISM (long prompts). DetailMaster is confirmed ICML 2026. CVPR 2026 adds HiCoGen. On the user's second interest, **SAP (contextual contradiction) is confirmed CVPR 2026 (poster)**. Beyond SAP, conflict and prior violation appear mainly as rare-concept blending (AAPB, CVPR), negative-prompt guidance (VSF, ICLR) and logic/negation composition calculi (ICLR, ICML). There is still no accepted paper on *resolving logically incompatible requirements in long prompts*. I found no 2026-H1 accepted paper that measures unrequested additions (precision) as a primary metric. OSPO (CVPR) is the only one that frames its target as "object hallucination" in generation.

### Cited Findings

#### Status updates for papers in the earlier report
- **DetailMaster → ICML 2026 (regular poster) [V].** The ICML JSON gives decision "Accept (regular)", poster 62789. The arXiv 2505.16915 comment reads "accepted by ICML2026". The ICML abstract says weak encoders "struggle to preserve syntactic dependencies" and that diffusion models "suffer from attribute leakage under detail-intensive conditions". It also says high fidelity needs "expanded prompt limits and long-prompt training" together. This settles the earlier note's ICLR-withdrawn vs ICML conflict. Interests: (1). — [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62789); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16915)
- **SAP "Image Generation from Contextually-Contradictory Prompts" → CVPR 2026, poster (no Highlight badge) [V].** It is on the CVF Open Access page, by Huberman, Patashnik, Dahary, Mokady, Cohen-Or, arXiv 2506.01929. The CVF abstract defines "contextual contradiction" as a case where "one concept implicitly negates another due to entangled associations". The method is an LLM-built stage-aware proxy-prompt sequence. The arXiv comments field shows no venue. Interests: (2). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Huberman_Image_Generation_from_Contextually-Contradictory_Prompts_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **OSI "Diagnosing and Correcting Concept Omission in Multimodal Diffusion Transformers" → ICML 2026 (regular) [V].** arXiv 2605.14270, comment "Accepted to ICML 2026". The abstract says linear probing on text tokens reveals an "omission signal" for absent concepts, and that amplifying it fixes omission on FLUX.1-Dev and SD3.5-M "even in extreme scenarios". The earlier report already has its numbers (FLUX multi-object 0.47→0.61; 6 objects 0.18→0.40). Interests: (1), (S). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/60974); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14270)
- **PRISM "Long-Text-to-Image Generation via Compositional Prompt Decomposition" → ICLR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2604.18258, comment "Accepted to ... ICLR 2026". A lightweight module extracts components from the long prompt. Per-component noise predictions are combined by energy-based conjunction. It reports +7.4% over baselines on prompts over 500 tokens. Interests: (1). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007837); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18258)
- **T2I-CoReBench "Easier Painting Than Thinking" → ICLR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2509.03516, comment "Accepted to ICLR 2026". It has 1,080 prompts, about 13,500 checklist yes/no questions and a 12-dimension taxonomy (instance/attribute/relation × deductive/inductive/abductive), and evaluates 38 models. Composition "remains limited in high compositional scenarios" and reasoning "lags even further behind". Interests: (1), (2) partly. — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007931); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03516)
- **GRPO-Guard → CVPR 2026 Highlight [V].** arXiv 2510.22319. It reports "implicit over-optimization": the proxy reward rises while image quality and text alignment fall. The fix is ratio normalization plus gradient reweighting across timesteps. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wang_GRPO-Guard_Mitigating_Implicit_Over-Optimization_in_Flow_Matching_via_Regulated_Clipping_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

#### NEW — omission / many-object / long-prompt (interest 1)
- **CO3 "Steer Away From Mode Collisions: Improving Composition in Diffusion Models" — ICLR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2509.25940, comment "Accepted to ICLR 2026"; v1 was titled "CO3: Contrasting Concepts Compose Better". The hypothesis is that a prompt like "a cat and a dog" drifts into mixed modes that overlap a single strongly learned concept, so the other concept is "missing, faint, or colliding". It is a training-free corrective sampler that steers away from those single-concept-dominated modes. Key numbers on SDXL two-concept prompts, BLIP-VQA: Animal–Animal 0.695→0.744, Animal–Object 0.865→0.888, Object–Object 0.493→0.515 (ToMe beats it on O-O, 0.644). On T2I-CompBench Complex, ImageReward 0.308→0.441 and human eval 0.598→0.803. No quantitative missing-concept rate is reported, and extra objects are not quantified. Tested on SDXL and PixArt-Σ only (no FLUX/SD3). Interests: (1), and (2) because the mechanism is "dominance by the strongly learned concept". — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009822); [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25940v2)
- **"When Do Diffusion Models Learn to Generate Multiple Objects?" — ICML 2026 (regular) [V-venue; no arXiv found].** It introduces *mosaic* (Multi-Object Spatial relations, AttrIbution, Counting), a controlled dataset generator, and trains diffusion models from scratch on it. Findings: "scene complexity plays a dominant role rather than concept imbalance". Counting is "uniquely difficult to learn in low-data regimes". Compositional generalization "collapses as more concept combinations are held out". Interests: (1), (S, counting). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65769)
- **Divide-and-Denoise: A Game-Theoretic Method for Fairly Composing Diffusion Models — ICML 2026 *spotlight* [V-venue; arXiv not checked].** It coordinates several single-concept T2I diffusion models. At each step it divides the sample into regions under game-theoretic fairness criteria, and the assigned model denoises each region. It reports outperforming baselines on GenEval and other metrics, "resolving common failures like missing objects and mismatched attributes". Interests: (1), (S). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65813)
- **HiCoGen — CVPR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2511.19965; the arXiv comment gives no venue. It targets "concept omission, confusion, and poor compositionality" on complex hierarchical prompts with a "Chain of Synthesis". An LLM splits the prompt into minimal units that are synthesized iteratively, each step conditioned on the previous image. RL uses a "Decaying Stochasticity Schedule", with a theoretical claim that diversity is maximized when stochasticity is concentrated early. A hierarchical reward scores global, subject and relation levels. It introduces the HiCoPrompt benchmark. No numbers appear on the abstract pages. Interests: (1). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Yang_HiCoGen_Hierarchical_Compositional_Text-to-Image_Generation_in_Diffusion_Models_via_Reinforcement_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **CLIP Is Shortsighted: Paying Attention Beyond the First Sentence (DeBias-CLIP) — CVPR 2026 *Highlight* [V].** arXiv 2602.22419. Long captions, human- or LLM-written, usually open with a one-sentence summary. This acts as a training shortcut that concentrates attention on the first sentence and early tokens. The fix removes the summary sentence, subsamples sentences and pads tokens. It is a drop-in replacement for Long-CLIP and is "less sensitive to sentence order permutations". This is an encoder-side position-bias result relevant to long-prompt omission (gap #1 in the earlier report), though it is evaluated on retrieval, not T2I. Interests: (1). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Lavoie_CLIP_Is_Shortsighted_Paying_Attention_Beyond_the_First_Sentence_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **SpatialGenEval "Everything in Its Place: Benchmarking Spatial Intelligence of T2I Models" — ICLR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2601.20354, comment "Accepted by ICLR 2026". It has 1,230 "long, information-dense prompts" across 25 scenes, each with 10 spatial sub-domains and 10 multiple-choice QA, evaluated on 23 models. Higher-order spatial reasoning is the main bottleneck. Fine-tuning on the SpatialT2I dataset (15.4K pairs) gives +4.2/+5.7/+4.4% on SDXL/UniWorld-V1/OmniGen2. Interests: (1, dense prompts), spatial. — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008393)
- **FLUX-Reason-6M & PRISM-Bench — ICLR 2026 poster [V-venue].** It has 6M FLUX images with 20M bilingual captions and Generation-CoT. PRISM-Bench has seven tracks, including a "Long Text" track, and evaluates 19 models. Interests: (1). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008508)
- **Follow the Flow: On Information Flow Across Textual Tokens in T2I Models — ACL 2026 (long) [V-venue].** By Kaplan, Toker, Reif, Belinkov, Schwartz. Activation patching shows an item's information is usually concentrated in one or two of its tokens; for "Golden Gate Bridge", the token "Gate" suffices. Items mostly stay isolated: in "a green dog", "dog" carries no "green". Cross-item flow sometimes causes misreadings, e.g. "a pool by a table" → "pool" encodes "pool table". Simple encoding-stage interventions improve alignment. Interests: (1, encoder-level cause), (S, binding). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1575/)

#### NEW — conflict / prior-violation / rare / negation / logic (interest 2)
- **AAPB "Adaptive Auxiliary Prompt Blending for Target-Faithful Diffusion Generation" — CVPR 2026 poster [V].** arXiv 2603.19158. For target concepts in low-density regions of the training distribution, it blends an auxiliary anchor prompt with the target prompt. The blend coefficient is closed-form, derived from Tweedie's identity, and is set per step. It generalizes R2F-style fixed prompt alternation, with gains on RareBench and FlowEdit. Interests: (2, prior-violating/rare). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Lee_Adaptive_Auxiliary_Prompt_Blending_for_Target-Faithful_Diffusion_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **VSF "Value Sign Flip" negative guidance — ICLR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2508.10931].** It flips the sign of attention *values* from the negative prompt in MM-DiT (SD3.5-Turbo, FLUX-Schnell) and in cross-attention models (Wan). It introduces **NegGenBench**. Negative-adherence scores: 0.420 (quality setting) / 0.545 (strong setting), vs 0.320–0.380 for prior few-step methods and 0.300 for CFG in non-few-step models. This is negative-*prompt* (exclusion) adherence, not in-prompt linguistic negation. Interests: (2, negation), (S, suppressing unwanted objects). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009083)
- **"Composition of Pretrained Diffusion Models: A Logic-Based Calculus" — ICLR 2026 poster [V-venue].** It shows that product/mixture score composition has "limited mode coverage, biased sampling, instability under negation queries" and breaks idempotency and distributivity. It proposes fuzzy-logic (Dombi) conjunction, disjunction and negation operators, including XOR, with Feynman-Kac correctors. Tested on Stable Diffusion and molecules. Interests: (2, formal handling of negation/conflict). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011058)
- **LOGDIFF "Logical Guidance for the Exact Composition of Diffusion Models" — ICML 2026 (regular) [V-venue; arXiv 2602.05549 title-matched].** It gives an exact Boolean calculus. If a formula compiles to a circuit whose conjunctions combine conditionally independent subformulas, and whose disjunctions combine independent or mutually exclusive ones, exact guidance follows from atomic scores plus posteriors. It also proposes a hybrid of classifier guidance and CFG. Interests: (2). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64380)
- **Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance ($g^{car}$) for flow models under compositional rewards — ICML 2026 [V-venue].** When multiple guidance constraints are composed, off-manifold drift "scales severely with gradient misalignment". The method detects and resolves gradient conflicts. It is validated on synthetic data, image editing and planning, not T2I prompts specifically. Interests: (2, conflicting objectives, an analogy at the guidance level). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65470)
- **"Attention, May I Have Your Decision? Localizing Generative Choices in Diffusion Models" — CVPR 2026 poster [V-venue; arXiv 2604.06052, matched by title in arXiv search].** For underspecified prompts, the model's *implicit* choices are localized principally in self-attention layers, not cross-attention, as found by attribute-separability probing. The ICM steering method intervenes on a few such layers, applied to debiasing. Interests: (2, how models resolve unspecified/competing attributes). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Zaleska_Attention_May_I_Have_Your_Decision_Localizing_Generative_Choices_in_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **Temporal Concept Dynamics via Prompt-Conditioned Interventions (PCI) — ICLR 2026 poster [V-venue; arXiv 2512.08486 title-matched].** It defines Concept Insertion Success (CIS) as the probability that a concept inserted at timestep t survives to the final image. Different models and concepts have different favorable phases. This is a tool for measuring *when* a concept locks in, relevant to SAP-style stage-aware conflict resolution. Interests: (2), (1). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011061)
- **Causal-Adapter (ICML 2026) [V-venue]** is about counterfactual *image editing* via structural causal models, with attribute interventions that preserve identity. It is not about prompt-level counterfactual worlds and has low relevance. — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61202)
- **RAIGen (ICML 2026) [V-venue]** uses Matryoshka SAEs to discover rare attributes encoded in SD/SDXL and can amplify them. It is tangential to prior-violation. — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61599)
- **Breaking Semantic Boundaries: Distribution-Guided Semantic Exploration for Creative Generation (DisTok) — CVPR 2026 *Oral* (Oral Session 3A: Generative Diffusion Modeling) [V-venue].** It is about creative out-of-distribution concept synthesis. It counts as "prior-violating" only in the creativity sense, not faithfulness. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Feng_Breaking_Semantic_Boundaries_Distribution-Guided_Semantic_Exploration_for_Creative_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

#### NEW — object hallucination / precision, binding, leakage, counting (S)
- **OSPO "Object-Centric Self-Improving Preference Optimization" — CVPR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2506.02015].** This is a self-improving preference optimization for unified MLLM generators. It builds object-centric preference pairs with no external data or models, and uses attention-based object masks with an object-weighted SimPO loss. It claims to "reduce object hallucination". This is the only 2026-H1 accepted paper I found that names *object hallucination* in generation as its target, though it appears to mean wrong attributes and relations rather than extra unrequested objects. Interests: (S). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Oh_OSPO_Object-Centric_Self-Improving_Preference_Optimization_for_Text-to-Image_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **DeLeaker — ICLR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2510.15015].** By Ventura, Toker, Patashnik, Belinkov, Reichart. It is an optimization-free inference-time reweighting of attention maps that suppresses cross-entity interactions. It introduces **SLIM**, 1,130 human-verified semantic-leakage samples. Interests: (S, binding/leakage). — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009402)
- **Mitigating Noise-Induced Layout Priors for Object Counting — ICML 2026 [V-venue; PMLR v306 "gu26f"; no arXiv found].** The initial noise determines layout, and early cross-attention propagates it. The method is training-free: count-aware noise adjustment plus test-time optimization of early cross-attention. It reports beating count-control state of the art on single- and multi-category benchmarks. Interests: (S, counting). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64469); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/gu26f.html)
- **Alignment-Guided Score Matching — ICML 2026 *spotlight* [V-venue].** It shows the contrastive soft-token method SoftREPA "excessively penalize[s] negative pairs", which manifests as "over-counting and repetition", i.e. extra instances. Folding the alignment guidance into the score-matching objective gives ">35% improvement in counting accuracy on GenEval" on SD1.5/SDXL/SD3. This is a rare explicit documentation of an alignment method *causing* extra objects. Interests: (S, counting/extras). — [ICML](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/60920)
- **DisCo "Resolving the Identity Crisis in T2I Generation" — CVPR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2510.01399].** GRPO with a compositional reward that penalizes duplicate faces and enforces person count. It reaches 98.6% Unique Face Accuracy on the DiverseHumans testset and claims to beat Gemini and GPT-Image. Interests: (S, counting/duplication of people). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Borse_Resolving_the_Identity_Crisis_in_Text-to-Image_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **NUMINA "When Numbers Speak" — CVPR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2604.08546].** This is *text-to-video* counting: it selects discriminative attention heads to get a countable latent layout, then guides cross-attention. Counting accuracy rises by up to +7.4% on Wan2.1-1.3B and +4.9/+5.5% on 5B/14B on the new CountBench. Interests: (S). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Sun_When_Numbers_Speak_Aligning_Textual_Numerals_and_Visual_Instances_in_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **HARoPE — CVPR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2510.10489].** It is a head-wise adaptive RoPE, using an SVD-parameterized linear map before the rotary mapping. It is motivated by RoPE limits on "fine-grained spatial relation modeling, color cues, and object counting". Tested on FLUX/MMDiT. Interests: (S). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Li_Head-wise_Adaptive_Rotary_Positional_Encoding_for_Fine-Grained_Image_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **Circuit Mechanisms for Spatial Relation Generation in DiTs — CVPR 2026 *Highlight* [V; arXiv 2601.06338].** It trains DiTs from scratch on two-object attribute+relation prompts. With random text embeddings, a two-stage circuit uses two cross-attention heads that separately read the relation and the object attributes. With T5, the DiT instead reads fused relation+object information from a single token. In-domain accuracy is similar, but out-of-domain robustness differs. Interests: (S, binding), mechanism. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wang_Circuit_Mechanisms_for_Spatial_Relation_Generation_in_Diffusion_Transformers_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **LayerBind "Layer-wise Instance Binding for Regional and Occlusion Control in T2I DiTs" — CVPR 2026 poster [V; arXiv 2603.05769].** It observes that layout and occlusion are fixed at a very early denoising stage. It builds per-instance branches through MM-DiT joint attention, fuses them by layer order at an early step, and then applies layer-wise "semantic nursing". Training-free. Interests: (S, multi-instance). — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Chen_Layer-wise_Instance_Binding_for_Regional_and_Occlusion_Control_in_Text-to-Image_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

### Inferences
- The omission literature has **moved from "attention-loss on UNet" to diagnosing mode/representation causes**. Examples: CO3's mode collisions, OSI's omission signal in text tokens, PCI's concept-insertion timing, Follow the Flow's token-level encoding, and DeBias-CLIP's first-sentence shortcut. Several of these (CO3, SAP, the earlier report's DominanceBench) independently attribute the missing object to *dominance by a strongly learned concept*. This supports the earlier report's hypothesis that long-prompt omission and conflict-loss are the same mechanism.
- DeBias-CLIP (position bias in long captions) plus DetailMaster (syntactic-dependency loss) is the closest accepted evidence for a **position effect** in long-prompt omission. No accepted paper does the causal clause-permutation test on T2I outputs, so gap #1 of the earlier report remains open.
- "Extra objects" appear mostly as a *side effect* documented by alignment methods: Alignment-Guided Score Matching documents over-counting and repetition from contrastive tokens, and the earlier report notes OSI's over-generation caveat. No accepted 2026-H1 paper proposes a precision metric for unrequested objects.

## Q2. What methodological themes dominate (MM-DiT attention steering, GRPO/RL with verifier rewards, think-then-generate unified models, test-time scaling, benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
By count, **GRPO-style RL with decomposed or verifiable rewards** is the dominant theme at CVPR 2026 and ICML 2026. **Reasoning/think-then-generate unified models** come second, and **test-time scaling with requirement checklists** third. **Training-free attention/guidance steering in MM-DiT** remains the main tool for omission, binding and counting specifically. **Benchmarks** shifted toward dense or long prompts with checklist QA.

### Cited Findings
- **RL / GRPO with structured or verifiable rewards:**
  - CVPR 2026: HiCoGen (hierarchical global/subject/relation reward), CompGen (curriculum GRPO), CGPO (reward-variance curriculum; arXiv 2605.17807), DisCo (count+identity reward), SpatialReward (detector + VLM CoT verifiable reward), SOLACE (intrinsic self-confidence reward; arXiv 2603.00918), GvU (understanding-branch intrinsic reward for UMMs; arXiv 2603.06043), PromptEnhancer (rewriter RL with AlignEvaluator over a failure-mode taxonomy; its abstract cites "attribute binding, negation, and compositional reasoning"), PromptLoop (step-wise prompt RL from latents; arXiv 2510.00430), plus GRPO-Guard and DiverseGRPO (both Highlights). — [CVF listing](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/CVPR2026?day=all)
  - ICML 2026: AlphaGRPO's DVReward, where an LLM decomposes a request into atomic verifiable questions and an MLLM judges them (arXiv 2605.12495, comment "ICML2026"); Pair-GRPO (FocusDiff); R³-Refiner GRPO (arXiv 2605.19639); StructMAR, which uses GRPO against detector layout metrics (COCO-MIG 61.7 ISR). — [ICML AlphaGRPO](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61792); [ICML StructMAR](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65257)
  - ICLR 2026: GoT-R1, RePrompt, TempFlow-GRPO, BranchGRPO, TreeGRPO, PCPO, plus DiffusionNFT as an ICLR 2026 **Oral**. — [ICLR JSON](https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2026-orals-posters.json)

### Inferences
- The two user interests map onto different method families. **Omission** is attacked mainly by training-free steering (OSI, CO3) and decomposition (PRISM, HiCoGen, Divide-and-Denoise). **Conflict/prior-violation** is attacked by stage-aware or blended prompting (SAP, AAPB) and formal composition logic (Dombi calculus, LOGDIFF). The RL/verifier wave optimizes holistic or checklist rewards that score *presence of requested items*. None of the reward designs I read penalizes unrequested objects. That keeps the earlier report's concern about reward hacking via extra objects open and untested.
- Verifiable decomposed rewards (AlphaGRPO's DVReward, SpatialReward, HiCoGen) are the natural host for a precision term ("no other salient objects") or a conflict-resolution term. This is an open design opportunity.

### Gaps
- I did not systematically count papers per theme across all ~4,000 CVPR titles. The theme ranking is based on my keyword-filtered shortlist (~120 CVPR, ~127 ICLR/ICML candidates).
- Most RL papers report GenEval/T2I-CompBench/DPG aggregates. Whether their gains come from omission vs binding vs counting sub-scores was not extracted.
